SEO proposal · November 2026

Stashbee began 2026 up 19% on last year. Since July, organic clicks have been down every month.

The gap widened to −15% in October. This is a proposal for an SEO audit, strategy and roadmap that puts the right page in front of the right searcher, in Google and in AI answers.

Prepared for Stashbee by Wayne Burden, DOTDW

Proof point three · The template and the listings

The location pages hide their inventory from Google, and the listings spend the crawl budget instead.

What Google sees on a location page

  • Every location template loads its map-and-listings module after the page, by script, so the HTML Google receives has no listings: Manchester self storage, London parking and Liverpool lock-ups alike. The London parking hub earned no impressions for its car parks’ names in July–October; the car parks rank 8–12 on their own listing URLs.
  • Structured data stops at Product and breadcrumbs. Nothing tells Google what is on the page: no ItemList of the spaces shown, no FAQ markup, no facility type for the self-storage operators that are real places.
  • Google’s Knowledge Graph entity for Stashbee is one office: “Storage facility in London”, 95 reviews, “people also search for” letting agents. JustPark is a “Technology company” with founder and founding date. The homepage’s Organization markup gives a different address from the Business Profile.
  • Boilerplate on every page: a dozen H2s, the same partner, how-it-works and hosting sections, and generic copy linking to national pages rather than the city.

02 Evidence · 09

This is the mechanical cause behind the first two slides. Schema, if asked: the existing Product and Offer markup is fine and stays; the audit reviews what to add. The options it will specify: Organization on the homepage with one address matching the Business Profile and sameAs to Companies House and the profiles, referenced from every page as the provider; BreadcrumbList plus an ItemList of the listings actually shown on each location page, and FAQPage where the FAQ is visible; and, where a listing is a self-storage operator’s facility, SelfStorage as the thing offered, which also feeds the entity work. Knowledge Graph: Stashbee has only a Business Profile entity for its office, no corporate entity, and the related-entity neighbourhood is letting agents; JustPark and Safestore have corporate entities with founder, founding date and the right neighbours. That is the entity gap AI answers inherit. A location page that cannot show its inventory in HTML ranks on its boilerplate alone, which is why the same template wins where the static copy happens to be stronger and loses where it is not. Meanwhile the listings absorb the crawl and the links and give back almost nothing.

Why

Three structural causes inside the site, and one shift in the market that punishes them.

01

The template

Listings invisible in the HTML, the same copy on every town page with the name swapped, no structured data describing what is on the page, and a listings module that loads after the page. Google ranks the static shell, so the page with the better shell wins regardless of what is for rent there.

02

The architecture

Up to five URL levels (category, county, town, district) spread thin inventory across 4,900 location pages whose location signals are too weak for Google to pick one page per place. Hence Bishopston for Bristol and Headingley for Leeds, not the city page; up to eight Stashbee pages in one city’s top 20; a Barnsley parking page ranking for “garage to rent near me”; and 8,300 pages shown this year without a click.

03

Crawl budget and the entity

Listings are 57% of pages and links for 3% of clicks; hubs link to the non-canonical URL; 2,322 enquiry URLs and 1,171 orphaned listings are crawled; sitemaps carry fake dates. And Google’s own panel files Stashbee under letting agents, so AI answers have no storage entity to cite.

The market: Google and AI Search now answer local intent themselves

A local pack sits above the results in 80% of the city searches we ran and an AI Overview in 46% (68% for garages). The blog lost 73% of its clicks while mostly holding its positions, because the answer now appears before the link. In every local marketplace we have studied, the organic demand that is left concentrates on list pages built from live inventory and structured data, not on thousands of thin location pages. Stashbee has the pages and the listings; the pages need to be built from them, and AI Search needs an entity it can name.

Sources: Search Console, full crawl, 300 city-level searches, 40 AI answers, Nov 2026.

The proposal

Three deliverables in four weeks: an audit that explains, a strategy that chooses, and a roadmap your developers can build from.

Deliverable 1

Audit

Site-wide, effort against impact. Eight workstreams, each answering one commercial question with Stashbee’s own data: why the lock-up hubs fell, whether self storage is worth the build, which location pages earn their place, how listings feed them, how the pages load, and how Stashbee shows up in AI answers. Hygiene items go in an appendix, not the headline.

Deliverable 2

Strategy

The decisions, ranked on effort against expected traffic and revenue: which segments to lead with, which pages to consolidate, how inventory and listings should be indexed, what the location template and schema become, and what to stop doing. Self storage gets its go/no-go here.

Deliverable 3

Roadmap

A sequenced backlog sized against your release cadence: sprint by sprint, each item with its effort, its expected impact, acceptance criteria and the measurement that proves it worked. Written as tickets, so the first sprint can start the week after playback.

Fixed scope, fixed fee. Delivery sprints are priced separately after the roadmap.

Reframed after review as hypotheses to test, not findings. Each area is framed as a question so the client can see the audit is not a tool export. Workstreams 3, 4 and 6 are one argument: which location pages should exist, the listings that justify them, and content assembled from those listings (counts, prices, sizes, ratings, nearby places) in place of the same prose on every town page.
