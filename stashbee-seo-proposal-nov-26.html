SEO proposal · November 2026

Stashbee began 2026 up 19% on last year. Since July, clicks from Google search have been down every month.

The gap widened to −15% in October. This is a proposal for an SEO audit, strategy and roadmap that puts the right page in front of the right searcher, in Google and in AI answers.

02 Evidence · 08

Proof point three · The template and the listings

The location pages hide their inventory from Google, and Google’s crawling goes on the listings instead.

What Google sees on a location page

  • Every location page loads its map-and-listings module by script after the page, so the page Google reads has no listings: Manchester self storage, London parking and Liverpool lock-ups alike. The London parking page was never shown for its car parks’ names in July–October; the car parks rank 8th to 12th on their own listing pages.
  • Structured data (the code that labels a page’s content for Google) stops at Product and breadcrumbs. Nothing tells Google what is on the page: no list of the spaces shown, no FAQ markup, no business-type label for the self-storage operators that are real places.
  • Google’s record of Stashbee (its Knowledge Graph entry) is one office: “Storage facility in London”, 95 reviews, “people also search for” letting agents. JustPark is a “Technology company” with founder and founding date. The homepage’s company markup gives a different address from the Google Business Profile.
  • The same filler on every page: a dozen headings, the same partner, how-it-works and hosting sections, and generic copy linking to national pages rather than the city.

02 Evidence · 10

Why

Three structural causes inside the site, and one shift in the market that punishes them.

01

The template

Listings invisible in the HTML, the same copy on every town page with the name swapped, no structured data describing what is on the page, and a listings module that loads after the page. Google ranks the fixed parts of the page, so the page with the better fixed parts wins regardless of what is for rent there.

02

The architecture

Up to five URL levels (category, county, town, district) spread thin inventory across 4,900 location pages that look too alike for Google to pick one page per place. Hence Bishopston for Bristol and Headingley for Leeds, not the city page; up to eight Stashbee pages in one city’s top 20; a Barnsley parking page ranking for “garage to rent near me”; and 8,300 pages shown this year without a click.

03

Crawling and Google’s record of Stashbee

Listings are 57% of pages and links for 3% of clicks; city pages link to duplicate listing URLs; Google is crawling 2,322 enquiry-form URLs and 1,171 listings nothing links to. And Google’s own record files Stashbee under letting agents, so AI answers have no storage business to cite.

The market: Google and AI Search now answer local intent themselves

A map box of local businesses sits above the results in 80% of the city searches we ran and an AI Overview in 46% (68% for garages). The blog lost 73% of its clicks while mostly holding its positions, because the answer now appears before the link. In every local marketplace we have studied, the search demand that is left concentrates on list pages built from live inventory and structured data, not on thousands of thin location pages. Stashbee has the pages and the listings; the pages need to be built from them, and AI Search needs a business it can name.

Sources: Search Console, full crawl, 300 city-level searches, 40 AI answers, Nov 2026.

The proposal

Three deliverables in four weeks: an audit that explains, a strategy that chooses, and a roadmap your developers can build from.

Deliverable 1

Audit

Site-wide, effort against impact. Eight workstreams, each answering one commercial question with Stashbee’s own data: why the main lock-up pages fell, whether self storage is worth the build, which location pages earn their place, how listings feed them, how the pages load, and how Stashbee shows up in AI answers. Housekeeping fixes go in an appendix.

Deliverable 2

Strategy

The decisions, ranked on effort against expected traffic and revenue: which segments to lead with, which pages to consolidate, how inventory and listings should be indexed, what the location template and structured data become, and what to stop doing. Self storage gets its go/no-go here.

Deliverable 3

Roadmap

A sequenced backlog sized against your release cadence: sprint by sprint, each item with its effort, its expected impact, acceptance criteria and the measurement that proves it worked. Written as tickets, so the first sprint can start the week after the playback session.

Fixed scope, fixed fee. Delivery sprints are priced separately after the roadmap.
